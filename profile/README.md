### 🪖 The Doctrine of the Hostile Terrain

Nobody drops a mercenary into paradise. You only get called when the terrain is unknown, hostile, and actively collapsing.

#### 1. Ignorance is Fine. Survival Instinct is Everything.
We don't pretend to know how every chaotic system works before we touch it. Real engineering isn't memorizing textbooks—it's being comfortable in the pitch black, reading the breadcrumbs, and knowing what questions to ask to map the battlefield.

#### 2. DIY or Die Trying
When you're stranded in a firefight, you don't wait for permission or an enterprise procurement cycle. If the right utility doesn't exist, we fabricate it out of whatever scraps are lying around—spare hardware, local scripts, and duct tape. If it works, it's brilliant. If it blows up, at least we went down swinging.

#### 3. Scavenger Engineering
Old systems leave clues: strange error traces, bizarre configuration quirks, forgotten logs, and weird legacy conventions. We scavenge those pieces, reverse-engineer the original intent, and use the system’s own weight to stabilize it.

#### 4. The Mission is Sacred
We don't debate theoretical perfection while the building is on fire. Triage the bleeding, stabilize the core, establish perimeter defenses, and make sure the people relying on the system survive another shift.
